02

Key challenges & pain points

1 · Pores and texture — the thing she sees first

  • Her pores, especially on the nose and cheeks, look larger every year, and nothing she buys makes them smaller. [E — the Instagram comment and lead testimonial on your own page are both pore-first]
  • Foundation stops sitting smoothly after a few hours; it settles into the pores and the lines around her mouth, so makeup makes the problem more visible by lunchtime.
  • Her skin looks fine in the mirror and bad in photos — particularly the front camera, particularly under the flat overhead light at work.

2 · Lost firmness and looking tired — the thing that actually hurts

  • The jawline and cheeks have softened. Nothing has "fallen," but the face she sees isn't the one she carries in her head.
  • Lines on the forehead, between the brows and around the mouth now stay visible at rest, not only when she smiles.
  • Colleagues, her mother, her kids read her as tired or stressed when she isn't. This is the wound. It isn't about beauty — it's about being misread.

3 · Product fatigue and distrust — the reason she doesn't buy

  • She has a drawer of half-used serums that promised exactly what yours promises. [E]
  • She can't reliably tell a real product from a dropshipped one, and she's been burned or knows someone who has. Swedish consumers are unusually alert to this. [I]
  • She is specifically afraid of retinol — peeling, redness, purging — and she has a job where she faces people. She cannot afford two weeks of a ruined face. [E — this is the single most-answered objection on your page, four separate times]
03

Goals & aspirations

Short-term · 1–3 months

  1. To see one visible thing change — usually the pores on her nose — so she can believe the rest is coming.
  2. To get through a workday without her makeup breaking up, and to leave the house some mornings without a full base at all.
  3. To find one product that works and then stop researching, stop scrolling, stop buying. The search itself is exhausting her.

Long-term · 1–5 years

  1. To age in a way she chose rather than one that happened to her — "look like myself, rested," never "look 30."
  2. To never need injectables. Not necessarily because she judges them, but because she wants to stay on the right side of a line she has drawn for herself.
  3. To feel unremarkable in a photograph — to be in the picture with her kids instead of the one taking it.
04

Emotional drivers & psychological insights

  • The gap between inside and outside. She doesn't feel 47. The mismatch between how she feels and how she is read is the actual pain — the pores are only where it shows.
  • She wants relief, not seduction. Copy written by men in this category defaults to "feel sexy again." That is not her driver. Hers is the absence of a negative: not being commented on, not being asked if she's tired, not flinching at a photo.
  • Jantelagen cuts both ways — the Swedish-specific insight. She wants to look better, but she does not want to look like she has tried. "Gjord" is an insult; vanity is faintly embarrassing to admit out loud. That makes a home routine emotionally safer than a clinic: nobody sees her go, nobody knows, and the result reads as "hon ser pigg ut" rather than "hon har gjort något." Discretion is your least obvious advantage and possibly your strongest.
  • She is a compliance risk and she knows it. She has abandoned routines before. Your week-by-week roadmap works on her precisely because it tells her what failure will look like — the purge — before she experiences it and quits.
  • Price is not the real objection. 349 kr is less than a dinner out. The objection is "another 349 kr wasted," which is a belief problem, not a money problem. The guarantee answers the money. Only the mechanism answers the belief.

Key emotional fears & deep frustrations

  • That she'll be visibly wrecked for two weeks and everyone at work will see it. The purge fear is the single thing standing between her and the buy button — and it is a social fear, not a medical one. Answer it socially.
  • That she is being taken for a fool again. Another Meta ad, another 349 kr, another bottle in the drawer. Every unsupported claim on your page feeds this fear instead of answering it.
  • That it's simply too late for her — that pores and lines at 47 are just age, and no cream changes age. This is the belief the mechanism has to break, and it's why "de som burit på porer och linjer längst ser ofta den tydligaste skillnaden" is the best sentence currently on your page.
07

Emotional & psychographic insights

  • She is solution-aware but mechanism-starved. She knows what retinol is; she's read the articles. What she has never been given is a credible reason why this one reaches where the others didn't. The 200 000 microneedles is that reason — and it should be the loudest thing on the page, not the fifth section down.
  • She trusts specificity and distrusts enthusiasm. Swedish consumers are unusually hype-resistant. "93% saw results!" reads as advertising. "Vecka 1 kan du få små utslag — det är normalt, det går över" reads as honesty, and honesty is what converts here. Your willingness to describe the bad weeks is a conversion asset. Lean harder into it.
  • She will buy the routine, not the bottle — but only after she believes the routine works. Which is why the 1/2/3-pack ladder is right, and why it should be argued after the proof rather than presented as a discount before it.
08

Typical emotional journey

A real sequence — each stage has to be reached before the next one lands, which is why the order of the sales page matters as much as its content.

01Awareness

It's a photo, usually. Someone tags her, or she opens the front camera by accident in bad light. She sees the pores on her nose and the lines around her mouth and registers, with a small drop in her stomach, that this is what other people have been seeing.

She doesn't act. She just starts noticing — in shop windows, on video calls, in the car mirror.

02Frustration

She starts buying. A serum from Lyko, something a colleague recommended, a sheet mask, an expensive cream at Christmas. She uses each one properly for two or three weeks, sees nothing, and drifts. The drawer fills up.

She starts to suspect the whole category is a con — and, simultaneously, that the problem might be her: her genes, her age, her skin. The frustration turns inward, which is why it goes quiet. She stops mentioning it.

03Desperation

Something forces the issue: a wedding, a milestone birthday, a divorce, a photo she can't unsee, a friend who suddenly looks great. She googles botox prices in her town, and either the number or the needle stops her. She reads about prescription retinol and finds the peeling stories.

She wants the result those things promise without the cost, the needles, or the two ruined weeks. This is the exact moment your ad reaches her — and why "professionella resultat hemma, skonsamt nog för känslig hud" is the right core promise: it's the only sentence that resolves all three constraints at once.

04Relief & commitment

She buys. Week one her skin tingles and she nearly stops — this is where you lose or keep her, and the roadmap is what keeps her. Somewhere around week two or three she notices her nose in the bathroom mirror and it is smoother, and that small verifiable win converts her from a buyer into a believer.

From there she stops evaluating and starts maintaining. She tells one or two people, quietly, and only if they ask what she's doing — because she'd rather they simply think she looks well.
