## 5.1 Retinaldehyde — what to verify

### The claim that looks strongest for us

A randomised, vehicle-controlled trial reportedly found **retinaldehyde and retinoic acid equally
effective at reducing wrinkles and skin roughness, with retinoic acid producing higher rates of
local irritation** — which reduced patient compliance.

If that holds on reading, it is the honest, evidence-based version of Bellenoor's core positioning:
comparable results to a prescription retinoid, better tolerated. It is stronger than anything
currently on the page, because it is real. **Verify first.**

### Papers to read

| Paper | Why it matters | Link |
|---|---|---|
| *Retinoids in the treatment of skin aging: an overview of clinical efficacy and safety* | Review; appears to be the source of the retinaldehyde-vs-retinoic-acid tolerability comparison | pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2699641/ |
| *Efficacy and safety of retinaldehyde 0.1% and 0.05% creams used to treat photoaged skin: a randomized double-blind controlled trial* | **Directly matches our 0.1% concentration.** Highest-priority read | researchgate.net/publication/324564073 |
| *The Clinical Efficacy and Tolerability of a Novel Retinaldehyde Serum with Firming Peptides to Improve Skin Texture and Signs of Photoaging* | Retinaldehyde **plus peptides** — the same formula shape as ours | pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39496127/ |
| *Use of Retinoids in Topical Antiaging Treatments: A Focused Review* (Adv Ther, 2022) | Covers nanoformulations / encapsulation | link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12325-022-02319-7 |
| *Evidence for the Efficacy of Over-the-counter Vitamin A Cosmetic Products…: A Systematic Review* (JCAD) | OTC-specific — the right evidence tier for a cosmetic claim | jcadonline.com/efficacy-of-over-the-counter-vitamin-a/ |

Also referenced in summary but not located: an **open trial of 0.05% retinaldehyde in 32 female
volunteers**, reporting reduced surface roughness and coarse wrinkling at 4 months. Find the primary
citation before using the numbers.

### ❌ Do not use: "11x faster" / "10x more bioavailable"

Both circulate widely in retinal marketing. Neither traced to a citable study in anything reachable.
One source states explicitly that **no clean head-to-head trial at matched concentrations exists**,
and that the speed advantage is inferred by comparing separate studies.

We do not currently make this claim. **Do not start.** The defensible version needs no numbers:

> Retinal omvandlas till hudens aktiva form i ett steg. Retinol behöver två.
> *(Retinal converts to the skin's active form in one step. Retinol needs two.)*

That is true, simple, and enough.

## 5.2 Spicules / microneedle delivery — what to verify

Mechanism as described across sources: mechanical friction drives the spicules into the superficial
stratum corneum; their tapered shape anchors them without deep dermal penetration; normal
exfoliation clears them within roughly 24–48 hours, and permeability is increased during that window.

**Note what this does and does not say.** The described action is in the **stratum corneum** — the
outermost layer — not the dermis. Our copy should describe getting *through the surface barrier*,
which is accurate and is exactly what the Ytfällan argument needs. It should not imply the needles
reach the dermis.

Leads:
- *Development of Sponge Microspicule Cream as a Transdermal Delivery System for Protein and Growth Factors from Deer Antler Velvet Extract* — a permeation-enhancement study — researchgate.net/publication/334148148
- US patents 10555896 and 11413237, "Skin penetration enhancing method / composition" — patents are not efficacy evidence, but they document the mechanism
- A cited study reporting improved hydration/barrier parameters and visible reduction in wrinkles, erythema and hyperpigmentation after biomicroneedle treatment — **primary citation not located**

⚠️ Most accessible material on spicules is published by ingredient suppliers and brands. Supplier
marketing is not evidence. Prioritise peer-reviewed permeation studies.

## 5.5 The critical question — the honest answer, pending verification

*Why would this work when the last five things she bought didn't?*

> Because the limiting factor for a topical active is not the molecule, it's whether it gets through
> the stratum corneum. Bellenoor pairs a vitamin A form that is one enzymatic step from the active
> form — and, on the evidence to be verified above, delivers results comparable to a prescription
> retinoid with less irritation — with a delivery system whose documented function is to breach that
> outer barrier and raise permeability for a window of hours.

Every clause in that sentence is checkable. That is the standard the final version has to meet.
