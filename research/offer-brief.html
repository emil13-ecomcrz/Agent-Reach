02

Level of consciousness

Verdict Low — with one Swedish caveat

She's a 47-year-old working woman on Facebook, not a biohacker. She isn't reading studies, she doesn't know what retinaldehyde is, and she will not follow a chemistry argument. Write at a 7th-grade level. Short sentences, concrete pictures, story before science.

The caveat that makes this market different: low consciousness normally licenses hype. Here it does the opposite — Swedish consumers are unusually hype-resistant, and overclaiming reads as a scam rather than as excitement.

So: low-consciousness structure, high-trust tone. Simple language, big emotion, calm delivery, honest numbers. The page that wins here is the one that says "vecka 1 kan huden bli lite torr" out loud.

03

Level of awareness

Verdict Solution-aware · ~30% problem-aware

She knows vitamin A is "the thing that works for wrinkles." She's tried a version of it, or been scared off it. She does not know Bellenoor, and she does not know retinal is different from retinol.

What this dictates about the lead: not a product lead ("Introducing Bellenoor Retinal Shot") and not a pure problem lead ("Do you have large pores?"). Solution-aware traffic converts on a mechanism lead — the new reason the thing she already believes in will finally work for her.

The problem-aware slice is served by the agitation block, not by the headline.

04

Stage of sophistication

Verdict Stage 4, tipping into 5

Every claim in this category has been made. "Reduces wrinkles," "smaller pores," "clinically proven," "professional results at home" — she has read all of it and none of it registers any more.

  • Stage 3 tactics are dead here. Saying "93%" louder makes it worse, not better.
  • Stage 4 is a new mechanism. This is your play — 200 000 microneedles is a genuine differentiator she has not heard before.
  • Stage 5 is identification. Where a market is exhausted even by mechanisms, you win on "this was written about me." Your page currently has zero identification. That's the gap.

Run 4 and 5 together: mechanism headline, identification body.

One useful asymmetry. Sweden runs roughly one stage behind the US and UK on DTC skincare advertising. A mechanism angle that's burned out in an American feed still has room here. That window closes — use it now.

05

Big idea

"Det var aldrig fel på ditt serum. Det kom bara aldrig in."

It was never your serum's fault. It just never got in.

  1. It absolves her. The avatar's frustration stage ends with her privately suspecting the problem is her — her age, her genes, her skin. This says: it wasn't you, and it wasn't even the products. It was physics. That release buys her attention immediately.
  2. It turns every past failure into evidence for you. The drawer of half-used serums stops being proof that nothing works and becomes proof that the mechanism matters.
  3. It leads straight into the UMS. The next sentence writes itself.
  4. It's defensible. Topical actives genuinely have poor penetration through the stratum corneum — a real limitation, not an invented villain. Verify §5.2

06

Metaphor

"Att smörja in en vanlig kräm är som att vattna en växt genom att spreja bladen. Det ser rätt ut. Det känns rätt. Men det kommer aldrig ner till rötterna."

Rubbing in an ordinary cream is like watering a plant by spraying the leaves. It looks right. It feels right. But it never reaches the roots.

Domestic, instantly legible to a 47-year-old, needs no science, and it maps exactly onto the mechanism: surface versus depth. It also carries the emotional payload — she was doing everything right.

Supporting metaphors

  • The locked door. Her barrier isn't broken, it's working — a door doing its job. The spicules aren't force, they're a key. Useful against the "is this safe?" objection: you're not breaking anything.
  • The message that never arrived. Retinal isn't a substance that fills lines — it's an instruction to rebuild collagen. Skin cells stop receiving it with age. This is the message finally getting through.
  • A shot, not a cream. Already carried by the product name. Deep, fast, dosed.
07

UMP — unique mechanism of the problem

NamedYtfällan — the surface trap

The story, in her language:

  1. 01
    Your skin's number one job is to keep things out. It's a barrier, and it's very good at it.
  2. 02
    From around 40, cell turnover slows. Dead cells sit longer at the surface, the walls around each pore lose their support, and pores start to look bigger and deeper.
  3. 03
    Here's the trap: the same slowdown that causes the problem also makes the barrier harder to get through. The older you get, the more you need the active — and the less of it ever reaches the layer where collagen is made.
  4. 04
    Which means every serum you've bought has been fighting the wrong battle. Not the wrong ingredient — the wrong depth.

Why this UMP fits the offer

  • It's new to her — nobody has explained her failures this way.
  • It isn't a conspiracy, so it survives Konsumentverket and Meta review.
  • It makes the solution inevitable rather than merely appealing. Once she accepts the trap, she needs delivery — and delivery is what you sell.

Verify Substantiation for the penetration limitation and for pore-wall and turnover changes with age — research §5.1, §5.2.

UMS — unique mechanism of the solution

NamedShot-effekten · 200 000 mikrokanaler
  1. 01
    The carriers. 200 000 microscopic spicules — silica needles finer than your pores — suspended in the formula. You feel a slight prickle. That's them working.
  2. 02
    The passage. They open temporary micro-channels through the surface layer. Not a wound — a doorway that closes on its own.
  3. 03
    The payload. Pure vitamin A as retinaldehyde — one step from the active form your skin uses, where ordinary retinol needs two. It arrives at the depth where collagen is built instead of evaporating off the top.
  4. 04
    The buffer. A 9-peptide complex and panthenol ride along, so you get retinal's force without prescription retinol's fallout.

"Samma vitamin. Helt annan adress."

Same vitamin. Completely different address.

Research §5.1–5.2 has to confirm the penetration evidence — and you must reconcile the contradiction already live on your page: particles described as "fem gånger mindre än en por" in one section and "16 gånger tunnare än dina porer" in another. One number, everywhere.

Guru

Unresolved — and the biggest liability in the offer. Your page names four experts (Dr. Anders Lindholm, Linnéa Sjögren, Malin Forsberg, Sofia Holmgren), each with a quote. If they're real, contracted advisors, this is an asset: strengthen it with credentials, photos and a link. If they aren't, they have to come out. Named medical professionals is the one category where invented proof stops being marketing. Verify — founder decision, this week

Three routes, in order of preference

  1. A real founder. For this market, a Swedish woman in her forties who built this because nothing worked on her own skin outperforms any doctor. It fits the low-consciousness, high-trust profile exactly, it costs nothing, and it can't be falsified. If that's your actual story, it's the strongest option available.
  2. One real dermatologist or hudterapeut, properly contracted and named, replacing all four. One verifiable expert beats four unverifiable ones.
  3. No guru — the mechanism is the hero. Entirely viable. Spicules and retinaldehyde carry the offer alone, and it removes the risk completely.
10

Discovery story

The frame: two things that already worked, that nobody had put together.

  • In France, dermatologists have used retinaldehyde for decades — the vitamin A one step from the active form, chosen because it delivers results with far less irritation than prescription retinoids. It never got the marketing budget retinol got, so most people have never heard of it. Verify §3.3
  • In Korea, clinics use spicules — microscopic silica needles from freshwater sponges, structures that have existed for hundreds of millions of years — to carry actives past the skin barrier without a needle. Verify §3.4

Nobody had put the gentle molecule inside the delivery system and made it a two-minute evening routine for a woman at home. That's the product.

Structure it as three beats: she tried everything and blamed herself → she found the two pieces → she put them together. Beat one is missing from your page entirely, and it's the beat that makes the reader stay.

Verify Only publish this as an origin story if it is your actual origin story. If it isn't, run it as an ingredient story — "två upptäckter, två världsdelar" — same information, no fabricated biography.

Funnel architecture

Now — fix the page you have

Meta cold → rebuilt PDP → checkout → post-purchase upsell → email/SMS.

  • Mechanism headline above the buy box
  • "Känner du igen dig?" agitation block before any chemistry
  • UMP → UMS → proof → roadmap, in that order
  • Bundle ladder argued after the proof, not before it
  • CTA repeated after every proof block
  • Klarna prominent at checkout — it's the default Swedish payment mindset
  • Post-purchase upsell: SPF 50, or a third tube at a one-time price
  • Email/SMS running the 8-week roadmap as a nurture sequence — day 0 how to start · day 3 the tingle is normal · day 7 don't quit · day 14 check your nose · day 30 increase · day 50 reorder

Next — the test that matters

Advertorial pre-sell against direct-to-PDP. For a solution-aware, sceptical, low-consciousness audience an advertorial usually wins, because the UMP needs room to land before any product appears.

Working title: "Därför fungerade aldrig ditt serum — förklarat av en hudterapeut." Verify — only if a real hudterapeut writes or approves it

Later — the leak you haven't plugged

There is no subscription. You sell a consumable, your own copy says stopping returns her to baseline within 2–3 months, and you're leaving the repeat revenue on the table. A "Hudskiftet" subscription — ships every 8 weeks, 15% off, cancel anytime — fits the offer's own logic. Retention here is worth more than any headline test.

  5. The advisory board. Verify or remove.
  6. Testimonial dates (feb–mars 2026) predate the product.
  7. Two "Steg 4"s in the how-to-use section · "det är A-Shot" in the safety FAQ, left over from another product name · "Vi bevisar dom" → "dem" in the credibility heading.

Assets that would move the number most, in order

  1. Real before/after photos with a week counter, from real customers, with consent
  2. Real Swedish UGC video — a woman in her forties, unpolished, in her own bathroom
  3. Cost-per-night math, blocked on knowing how long a tube lasts
  4. A "vecka 1 ser ut så här" honesty block — the thing no competitor will do

Strategic note. Your genuine edge in this market isn't the formula — Medik8 has a stronger one at four times the price.

It's that you are willing to tell a Swedish woman the truth about the first two weeks. In a Stage 4/5 market where every competitor is claiming effortless transformation, honesty is the differentiator that can't be copied without giving up the claim.

Build the offer on it.
