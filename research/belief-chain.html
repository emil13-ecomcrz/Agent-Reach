The six beliefs Maria has to hold before she buys — and the order she has to reach them in.

Source Avatar sheet + offer brief Beliefs 6 of 11 kept Version v1 · Sept 2026

Marketing changes what someone believes. These are the only six changes that matter for this product — everything else on the page is either evidence for one of them or a distraction from all of them.

The order is the argument. Each belief is only reachable once the one before it holds. Reordered, they stop being a chain and become a list.

Belief 01 · The unlock

I believe it was never my fault. The things I tried never got past the surface.

"Jag tror att det aldrig var mitt fel. Det jag testat har aldrig tagit sig förbi ytan."

This is the belief the whole campaign exists to install. Her frustration stage ends with her privately suspecting the problem is her — her age, her genes, her skin. Until that suspicion is lifted, she reads every claim as one more thing that won't work on her specifically.

It does three jobs at once: it absolves her, it explains her entire history of failure, and it makes delivery the thing that matters — which is the only thing you sell that nobody else does. It also quietly kills "it's too late for me," because if the cause was depth rather than age, age was never the obstacle.

Proven by
The Ytfällan argument: skin's job is keeping things out → turnover slows after 40 → the barrier gets harder to cross exactly when she needs the active most → every serum was fighting the wrong battle.
Without it
Nothing else lands. She reads the rest of the page as advertising.

Belief 02 · The mechanism

I believe this one actually gets pure vitamin A beneath the surface, where the others stopped.

"Jag tror att det här faktiskt kommer ner dit — rent A-vitamin, buret förbi ytan av 200 000 mikronålar."

Belief 01 opens a hole; this fills it. The mechanism has to be specific, physical and picturable — 200 000 spicules finer than her pores, opening temporary channels, carrying retinaldehyde to the depth where collagen is built.

Proven by
The four-step mechanism, the watering-the-leaves metaphor, and the prickle she feels on night one — a sensory proof she can verify herself, which is rare and valuable.
Without it
She accepts your diagnosis and goes shopping for any product that solves it. This is the belief that makes the argument end at you rather than at Medik8.

This belief is only as strong as the numbers behind it. The page currently gives two different pore ratios — "fem gånger mindre än en por" and "16 gånger tunnare än dina porer" — and no source for either. Fix that before this belief is asked to carry the sale.

Belief 03 · The safety gate

I believe it's strong enough to work and gentle enough that I can still go to work.

"Jag tror att den är stark nog att ge resultat och skonsam nog att jag kan gå till jobbet som vanligt."

Both halves belong in one belief, because separating them is what kills the sale — anything she believes is gentle, she assumes is weak. The retinaldehyde-plus-buffer story is what allows both to be true at once.

Proven by
0.1% retinaldehyde buffered with peptides and panthenol; an honest week-one description; and framing the purge as a social reassurance rather than a dermatological one — nobody will see it, start on a Friday, here is exactly what week one looks like.
Without it
She wants it and doesn't buy. This is objection #2 and the most common silent exit on the page.

Belief 04 · The proof horizon

I believe I'll be able to see it working, and I know roughly when.

"Jag tror att jag kommer att se att den fungerar, och jag vet ungefär när."

Her defining past experience is "I used it properly for three weeks, saw nothing, and drifted." She isn't afraid the product fails — she's afraid of being unable to tell. A named checkpoint converts that fear.

Proven by
The week-by-week roadmap, with pores first — the fastest visible win, and the one she can verify herself in her own bathroom mirror.
Without it
She buys and refunds, or buys once and never reorders. This belief protects margin more than it wins the first sale — but it also removes real hesitation at checkout.

Belief 05 · The trust floor

I believe this is a real company that will actually give me my money back.

"Jag tror att det här är ett riktigt företag som faktiskt betalar tillbaka om jag inte är nöjd."

Unglamorous and non-negotiable. She is buying an unknown brand from a Meta ad, and Swedish consumers are unusually alert to dropship. A guarantee she doesn't believe in is worth nothing.

Proven by
Org.nr, a real Swedish address, a named support email, and the return mechanics stated plainly: who pays return shipping, whether an opened tube is accepted, how long a refund takes. And by every claim on the page being one she can check.
Without it
The guarantee stops working as risk reversal, and the entire cost of belief 03 lands back on her.

Every unsourced statistic on the page works against this belief. The 93 / 89 / 91 / 94% figures and the "36 månader"-study claim don't just risk a regulator — they spend the credibility that belief 05 runs on.

Belief 06 · The commitment

I believe the real way to do this is the full course, and it starts tonight.

"Jag tror att det enda sättet att göra det här på riktigt är hela kuren — och att det börjar ikväll."

The purchase-shaping belief, and it must come last. Argued before the proof, it's a discount. Argued after, it's a conclusion she reaches herself: if results build over eight weeks and stopping returns me to baseline, one tube was never really the option.

Proven by
Your own roadmap and frequency block — which already make this case, and are currently positioned as information rather than as the reason to take the 3-pack.
Without it
She buys one tube, runs out mid-roadmap, and you've paid full acquisition cost for a third of the revenue.
Cuts

What didn't survive, and why

Five of the eleven beliefs from the offer brief were dropped or absorbed.

Cut beliefReason
"My pores and lines are real and getting worse"She already believes it — that's why she clicked. Marketing changes beliefs; this one needs no changing. It's the entry condition, not a belief to install.
"Vitamin A is the right ingredient, retinal the right form"Folded into 02. Standalone, it's category education that sends her to a cheaper retinal. It should only ever travel inside the delivery claim.
"It's not too late for me"Folded into 01. If the cause was depth rather than age, this is settled without a separate argument — and one belief that resolves two fears is stronger than two that each resolve one.
"It works on skin like mine"Folded into 03, where it belongs. It's a tolerance question wearing a suitability costume.
"Waiting costs me more than starting"Folded into 06. Urgency isn't a belief she holds about your product — it's the timing edge of a commitment she's already making.
Note

The one thing to notice

The live page never installs belief 01. It opens on the product and argues 02, 03 and 04 well.

So it is making a strong case to a woman who still half-suspects the problem is her — which is how a page full of true, well-written sections still converts at 2.2 ROAS.

Belief 01 is also the only one of the six that costs nothing to add. No new asset, no substantiation, no photography. Just the right 200 words in the right place, above the fold.
